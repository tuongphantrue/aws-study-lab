1 · Start here

The big idea: recovery objectives come before services

Backup and restore has low ongoing cost but long recovery. Pilot light keeps critical core components ready. Warm standby runs a complete but smaller environment. Multi-site active/active runs production capacity in multiple locations.

The correct choice is the least expensive strategy that still meets both the RTO and RPO.

2 · Step by step

What AWS does

  1. Classify workloads and define RTO and RPO with the business.
  2. Select backup, replication, and secondary-region capacity that meets those objectives.
  3. Automate failover, infrastructure creation, configuration, and data restoration.
  4. Test the recovery process regularly; an untested backup is not a recovery plan.
3 · Architecture example

Follow the request

Primary Region→Replication or backups→Secondary Region→Failover and scale-up→Recovered service

A service requiring recovery within minutes keeps a warm standby environment in another Region, replicates data continuously, and uses Route 53 or Global Accelerator for failover.

4 · When to use it

Choose it for these requirements

  • Choose backup and restore when hours of downtime are acceptable and cost must be minimal.
  • Choose pilot light when core data and minimal services should remain ready.
  • Choose warm standby for a complete, scaled-down environment and recovery in minutes.
  • Choose multi-site active/active for the lowest recovery time and highest cost.
5 · Exam tip

Words that should guide your choice

Look for the combination of recovery speed, acceptable data loss, and cost. Do not automatically choose the most resilient design if the question prioritizes low cost.

6 · Common mistake

Do not confuse these ideas

Multi-AZ protects against an Availability Zone failure inside one Region. It is high availability, not automatically a complete multi-Region disaster recovery strategy.

RememberRTO is time. RPO is data. Faster recovery means more running infrastructure.
